# 🚀 Lesson 93: Performance Review & Salary Adjustments

# Part 1: Resource Lesson (Deep Dive Theory)

## 1. 🎯 Executive Summary & Objectives
**Goal:** Master the language and strategic phrasing required to negotiate a salary adjustment or promotion during an annual or bi-annual performance review.
**Engineering Impact:** Engineers who can articulate their quantifiable impact, align their achievements with business goals, and confidently request appropriate compensation are more likely to achieve accelerated career growth and fair market valuation.

## 2. 🧊 Warming Up
**Q1: How do you typically prepare for a performance review?**
*Sample Answer:* I usually compile a "brag document" detailing my major PRs, architectural contributions, and mentorship efforts over the past year.

**Q2: What is the most challenging part of asking for a raise?**
*Sample Answer:* Finding the balance between being assertive about my value and remaining professional without sounding entitled or demanding.

## 3. ⚙️ Contrastive Analysis & Theory
**The Formula for a Compensation Conversation:**
1. **Anchor on Impact:** Start by summarizing your quantifiable achievements (e.g., reduced latency, delivered project X on time).
2. **Pivot to Market/Value:** Introduce data or the expansion of your role (e.g., taking on tech lead responsibilities).
3. **The Ask:** Clearly state your request (percentage, band increase, or specific number).
4. **Silence:** Stop talking and wait for their response.

**Key Phrases:**
- "Based on my contributions to..."
- "Given the expansion of my responsibilities into..."
- "I'd like to discuss bringing my compensation in line with..."

## 4. 🛠️ Real-World Engineering Context Comparison

| Context | B1/B2 (Junior/Mid) | C1/C2 (Senior/Staff) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Opening the discussion | "I want to talk about my salary because I worked hard." | "I’d like to dedicate some time today to discussing my career progression and compensation trajectory." |
| Presenting data | "I built the microservice." | "I architected the new microservice, which reduced system latency by 25% and saved the team 40 hours of manual effort per month." |
| Making the ask | "Can I get a 10% raise?" | "Given my transition into a de facto tech lead role and the market data for this tier, I'm looking for a salary adjustment of X to align with my current impact." |
| Handling pushback | "Okay, I understand." | "I understand there are budget constraints. Can we establish clear, measurable milestones for the next cycle to guarantee this adjustment?" |

## 5. 🎭 Role-Play Dialogue
**Context:** An engineering performance review between Alex (Senior Engineer) and Taylor (Engineering Manager).

**Taylor:** Alex, you've had a fantastic year. The migration to Kubernetes was flawless.
**Alex:** Thank you, Taylor. I'm really proud of how the team rallied around that migration. Since we're discussing my performance, I'd like to pivot slightly to compensation.
**Taylor:** Sure, what's on your mind?
**Alex:** Over the last two quarters, I've formally taken on the architectural design for the checkout pipeline and mentored two junior devs. Given this expansion of my scope and my consistent 'exceeds expectations' ratings, I'd like to discuss a base salary adjustment of 15% to align with a Staff Engineer market rate.
**Taylor:** I agree your scope has grown, but 15% is tough with current budget freezes.
**Alex:** I appreciate the transparency regarding the budget. If a base adjustment isn't feasible right now, could we explore equity refreshers or set a formal milestone plan for a mid-year cycle adjustment?

## 6. 💬 Let's talk about the role play
**Q1: Why did Alex mention mentoring and architectural design?**
To objectively justify the request by proving their scope of work has expanded beyond their current job grade.

**Q2: How did Alex respond to the budget constraint?**
Instead of giving up, Alex pivoted to alternative compensation (equity) and a future commitment (mid-year cycle).

## 7. 📚 Bilingual Technical Vocabulary

| English Word | English Definition | Vietnamese Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Quantifiable** | Able to be measured or expressed as a quantity. | Có thể định lượng |
| **Trajectory** | The path followed by a process or career over time. | Quỹ đạo / Lộ trình |
| **Constraint** | A limitation or restriction, often financial or temporal. | Ràng buộc / Hạn chế |
| **Refresher** | Additional equity or stock options granted to retain an employee. | Cổ phiếu bổ sung (giữ chân nhân viên) |
| **Feasible** | Possible to do easily or conveniently. | Khả thi |

## 8. 🚫 Common Anti-Patterns & Pitfalls
- **Anti-Pattern:** Using emotional arguments ("I need a raise because my rent went up").
  - **Correction:** Always anchor on business value ("My contributions generated X revenue/savings").
- **Anti-Pattern:** Giving a range instead of a specific number ("I want between $140k and $150k").
  - **Correction:** The employer will always pick the bottom of the range. Give a specific anchor number or percentage.

**1. Which phrasing is most appropriate for initiating a salary discussion during a review?**
- [ ] A. "I want to talk about getting more money because I worked hard."
- [ ] B. "Can you give me a 15% raise?"
- [ ] C. "I’d like to dedicate some time today to discussing my compensation trajectory in light of my recent contributions."

<details>

## 🏆 Section 5: Model C1 & C2 Executive Script Benchmarks

**C1 (Senior Technical Lead):**
"I appreciate the positive feedback on the database migration. Over the past year, my role has naturally expanded to include mentoring the junior devs and leading architecture design. I’d like to discuss adjusting my compensation to reflect these new responsibilities and align with market rates for a Senior level."

**C2 (Principal Architect / Executive):**
"Thank you for the performance evaluation. Looking at the quantifiable impact—specifically the 40% reduction in latency and the successful rollout of the new billing pipeline—it's clear my scope now firmly aligns with the Staff engineering tier. I’d like to open a dialogue about a commensurate base adjustment, or barring that due to current fiscal constraints, structure an equity refresher that reflects this retained value."

## 📊 Section 6: CEFR C1/C2 Self-Assessment Evaluation Rubric

| Criteria | B2 (Meets Expectations) | C1/C2 (Exceeds Expectations) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Objectivity** | Mentions hard work. | Relies exclusively on quantifiable metrics and business impact. |
| **Vocabulary** | Uses basic words (raise, want, need). | Uses executive lexicon (commensurate, trajectory, adjustment, scope). |
| **Negotiation Agility** | Accepts a "no" or budget constraint easily. | Pivots gracefully to alternative compensation or future commitments (off-cycle review). |
| **Emotional Control** | May sound demanding or defensive. | Maintains a collaborative, objective, and forward-looking tone. |
