## 🎯 Executive Summary & Objectives
The goal of this lesson is to master the language, frameworks, and negotiation strategies for discussing Equity and Restricted Stock Units (RSUs). As engineering compensation at the senior/staff level is heavily weighted toward equity, understanding how to communicate about vesting schedules, refreshers, and equity valuation is critical for maximizing Total Compensation (TC).

## 🧊 Warming Up
**Question 1:** Have you ever negotiated an equity package or RSU grant? What was the hardest part?
*Sample Answer:* "I negotiated options at a startup once, but understanding the real value versus paper value was confusing. I felt I didn't have the right vocabulary to push for more."

**Question 2:** Why do tech companies prefer granting RSUs over large cash sign-on bonuses?
*Sample Answer:* "RSUs act as 'golden handcuffs.' They retain talent over a 4-year vesting period and align the engineer's financial success with the company's performance."

## ⚙️ Contrastive Analysis & Theory
**Key Negotiation Levers:**
- **Initial Grant Size:** The total dollar amount or number of shares granted upon hire.
- **Vesting Schedule:** Typically 4 years, sometimes with a 1-year cliff (25% vests after 1 year, then monthly/quarterly).
- **Refreshers:** Additional grants given annually to retain top performers.
- **Sign-on Bonus as Equity Bridge:** Using cash to offset unvested equity left on the table at a previous employer.

**Grammar & Tone Focus:** 
- Use **conditional framing** ("If we can adjust the initial grant...")
- Use **anchoring phrases** ("Based on the market data for this level...")
- Maintain an **objective, collaborative tone**, focusing on "aligning value" rather than "demanding more."

## 🛠️ Real-World Engineering Context Comparison

| Junior/Mid-Level (B1/B2) | Senior/Staff (C1/C2) | Why C1/C2 is better |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "I want more RSUs because my current company pays me more." | "To make this move viable, I need us to bridge the gap on the unvested equity I'd be walking away from." | Focuses on the business logic ("bridge the gap") rather than a simple demand. |
| "Is the vesting schedule negotiable? Four years is too long." | "Could we explore a front-loaded vesting schedule or an accelerated one-year cliff to offset the initial risk?" | Uses precise financial terminology ("front-loaded", "accelerated"). |
| "I need 500 more shares." | "If we could adjust the initial equity grant to $250k, it would align perfectly with my compensation expectations for this scope of impact." | Anchors the request to "scope of impact" and uses dollar values rather than share count. |
| "Do you give more stock every year?" | "Can you walk me through the historical cadence and target ranges for annual equity refreshers at this level?" | Demonstrates knowledge of compensation cycles ("cadence", "refreshers", "target ranges"). |

## 🎭 Role-Play Dialogue
**Scenario:** A Staff Engineer (Alex) is negotiating their offer with a Tech Recruiter (Sarah) at a Tier-1 tech company. The base salary is strong, but the equity grant falls short of Alex's target.

**Alex:** "Sarah, thanks for sending over the details. I'm really excited about the team and the architecture challenges we discussed. I did want to talk about the equity component, though."
**Sarah:** "Of course, Alex. What are your thoughts on the RSUs?"
**Alex:** "The base and bonus look solid, but the initial equity grant of $400k over four years is a bit lower than I anticipated for a Staff role. Given the unvested equity I'm leaving on the table at my current company, I'd need the initial grant to be closer to $550k to make this move make sense financially."
**Sarah:** "I understand. That’s a significant bump. We’re currently mapping you to the mid-band for Staff. Let me ask the compensation team if we can flex on the equity."
**Alex:** "I appreciate that. If adjusting the core grant is challenging due to banding, I'd also be open to exploring a front-loaded vesting schedule or a sign-on bonus to bridge that initial year-one cliff."
**Sarah:** "That’s a helpful alternative. Let me take both options back to the comp committee and see what we can do."

## 📚 Bilingual Technical Vocabulary

| English Word | English Definition | Vietnamese Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RSU (Restricted Stock Unit)** | A form of compensation issued by an employer in the form of company shares. | Cổ phiếu thưởng nội bộ / Cổ phiếu hạn chế |
| **Vesting Schedule** | The timeline over which an employee earns the right to their RSUs. | Lịch trình giải ngân / Lịch trao quyền |
| **Cliff** | The initial period (usually 1 year) before the first portion of equity vests. | Thời điểm giải ngân đầu tiên |
| **Refresher** | Additional equity granted to existing employees to retain them. | Cổ phiếu thưởng định kỳ / Cấp thêm cổ phiếu |
| **Unvested Equity** | Shares granted but not yet earned or owned by the employee. | Cổ phiếu chưa được giải ngân |
| **Front-loaded Vesting** | A schedule where more shares vest in the earlier years (e.g., 33/33/22/12). | Lịch giải ngân dồn vào các năm đầu |
| **To bridge the gap** | To make up the difference between two amounts (e.g., using cash to cover lost equity). | Thu hẹp khoảng cách / Bù đắp phần chênh lệch |

## 🚫 Common Anti-Patterns & Pitfalls
- **Focusing only on the number of shares:** Avoid negotiating share counts without knowing the current strike price or valuation. Always negotiate based on the **dollar value** of the grant.
- **Ignoring the Vesting Cadence:** Assuming all 4-year schedules are equal. A company vesting monthly after a 1-year cliff is vastly superior to one vesting annually.
- **Revealing the lowest acceptable number:** Don't say "I'd accept anything over $100k." Instead, anchor higher: "Market data suggests this role commands $150k-$200k in equity."
- **Ultimatums:** Avoid "Give me X or I decline." Use collaborative framing: "To get me to a resounding yes, we'd need to look at..."

## 🏆 Section 5: Model C1 & C2 Executive Script Benchmarks

### C1 (Senior Engineer)
> "Thanks so much for the offer, I'm really excited about the team. Looking at the numbers, the base is great, but the equity is a bit lower than I anticipated. I'm currently holding about $80k in unvested RSUs that I'd be walking away from. To make this transition make sense for me financially, I'd be looking for an initial grant closer to $250k. Is there room to adjust the RSUs to bridge that gap?"

### C2 (Staff/Principal Architect)
> "I truly appreciate the offer and I'm very aligned with the architectural vision we discussed. From a total compensation perspective, the base is exactly where it needs to be. However, the equity component falls slightly short of market targets for this scope of impact, especially factoring in the unvested equity I’m leaving on the table. To get me to a resounding yes today, I'd need the core RSU grant adjusted to the $250k range. If the equity band is constrained, I'm highly open to exploring a sign-on bonus or a front-loaded vesting schedule to offset the initial year-one impact. How flexible can we be on those levers?"
